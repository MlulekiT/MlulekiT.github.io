Research programme

Understanding populations through health, households and change

My work sits at the intersection of demography, population health and health inequalities, with particular attention to the ways households, social relationships and population processes shape health across the life course.

Central question

How do population processes, social environments and unequal access to resources shape health across the life course?

01

Maternal, reproductive & early-life health

Maternal healthcare utilisation, sexual and reproductive health, adolescent health, newborn care and early-life outcomes across Sub-Saharan Africa.

02

Health inequalities & utilisation

Social and spatial inequalities in health, healthcare access, utilisation, barriers to care and population-level patterns of vulnerability.

03

Households, ageing & healthy longevity

Kinship, living arrangements, household structure, multimorbidity, functional limitation, care dependence and the social context of ageing.

04

Population dynamics & methods

Fertility, mortality, migration, population structure and spatial patterns, supported by quantitative methods for analysing African population data.

Scholarship

Evidence-driven research with an African population perspective

My scholarship is grounded in population-level evidence and quantitative analysis. I work primarily with nationally representative surveys, censuses and other large-scale population datasets.

A recurring concern across my work is how demographic evidence can be used to understand unequal health experiences without losing sight of the populations, households and social contexts behind the numbers.

Increasingly, this means bringing demographic thinking into population health questions and bringing population structure, households and intergenerational relationships into health research.

Methods & data

Demographic questions require demographic evidence

Quantitative methods are used not simply to model associations, but to understand population patterns, inequalities and change with appropriate attention to measurement and data quality.

01

Population data

Large-scale datasets provide the empirical foundation for examining population health and demographic change.

  • DHS
  • MICS
  • General Household Survey
  • Census data
  • National population surveys
02

Quantitative analysis

Statistical and demographic methods are selected according to the structure of the population process and research question.

  • Multilevel modelling
  • Survival analysis
  • Demographic estimation
  • Population projections
  • Spatial analysis
03

Measurement & evidence

Particular attention is given to how variables are defined, measured and interpreted within African population datasets.

  • Questionnaire-first interpretation
  • Metadata and variable assessment
  • Official-report validation
  • Measurement under uncertainty
  • Reproducible analysis

Profile

About my academic work

I am a Senior Lecturer in Population Studies and Demography at North-West University, where I work on demographic, population-health and social research questions with a focus on Sub-Saharan Africa.

My research interests have developed from maternal and reproductive health toward a broader population-health perspective that connects health with households, kinship, population dynamics and ageing.

Across these areas, I am interested in producing evidence that is methodologically rigorous, substantively relevant and attentive to the realities of African populations and data.
