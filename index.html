Research programme

Population, households, health and the life course

A population-health research programme focused on understanding how demographic processes, household relationships and social inequalities shape health across the life course in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Central question

How do demographic change, household relationships and social inequalities shape health across the life course in African populations?

01

Population Dynamics

Fertility, mortality, migration, ageing, population distribution, family dynamics and demographic change, supported by quantitative and spatial approaches.

02

Households & Kinship

Household composition, kinship, intergenerational relationships, living arrangements and the ways social structures shape population health and vulnerability.

03

Life-Course Health

Maternal, reproductive, adolescent, adult and older-person health, with attention to transitions, cumulative disadvantage and intergenerational processes.

Health Inequalities

Unequal access to and use of health services, social determinants, multimorbidity, disability and population-level patterns of health and healthcare utilisation.

Current work

Building an integrated population-health research programme

Current work develops a connected body of evidence on ageing, households, health inequalities and population dynamics in South Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa.

Ageing

Ageing, households & healthy longevity

Examining how living arrangements, kinship and household structure shape older people's health, vulnerability, care dependence and prospects for healthy ageing. Current work includes older-person household typologies, multimorbidity, functional limitation and integrated measures of ageing.

Households

Kinship, living arrangements & population health

Investigating households not simply as demographic units, but as social environments in which care, resources, dependence and intergenerational support are organised.

Health inequalities

Unequal health and healthcare utilisation

Analysing socioeconomic, geographic and demographic inequalities in health status, healthcare access and healthcare utilisation, with particular attention to populations experiencing structural disadvantage.

Sexual & reproductive health

Maternal, reproductive & adolescent health

Continuing established research on maternal healthcare utilisation, newborn and postnatal care, adolescent sexual and reproductive health, contraceptive use and related population-health inequalities.

Population dynamics

Demographic change and population processes

Applying demographic methods to fertility, mortality, migration, population structure, family dynamics and spatial population processes across Southern and Sub-Saharan Africa.

Measurement

African population data & measurement

Developing rigorous approaches to analysing complex population-health phenomena using African survey and census data, with an emphasis on transparent measurement, metadata, uncertainty and appropriate quantitative inference.

Academic profile

Demography with a population-health lens

I am a Senior Lecturer in Population Studies and Demography at North-West University, where my work focuses on population dynamics, population health and health inequalities in Sub-Saharan Africa.

My research has developed from an established programme in maternal, reproductive and child health toward a broader population-health perspective across the life course. Increasingly, I examine how households, kinship, demographic change and social inequalities influence health and wellbeing.

A central principle of my work is that population-health questions require both demographic reasoning and careful attention to the structures in which people live. This includes households, intergenerational relationships, communities and broader systems of inequality.

I primarily work with large-scale population and health datasets, including DHS, MICS, GHS, Census and national household survey data, using demographic and quantitative methods to generate evidence relevant to research, policy and planning.
