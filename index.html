Research Identity

Population health through a demographic and life-course lens

My research brings together population studies, demography and population health to understand how demographic processes, relationships, social structures and inequalities shape health across the life course.

Central research question

How do demographic processes, household relationships and social inequalities shape population health across the life course in Sub-Saharan Africa?

01

Maternal, Reproductive & Early-Life Health

Maternal healthcare, sexual and reproductive health, newborn care, childhood health and early-life population processes.

02

Health Inequalities & Utilisation

Social inequalities, healthcare access and utilisation, disability, vulnerability and population health outcomes.

03

Households & Population Dynamics

Household structure, kinship, family relationships, fertility, mortality, migration and changing population relationships.

04

Ageing & Healthy Longevity

Population ageing, multimorbidity, functional limitation, care dependence, social protection and healthy longevity.

Life Course

Connecting demographic processes across life stages

Rather than treating population health outcomes as isolated events, my work examines how experiences and relationships accumulate, interact and change across the life course.

Current Work

Research programmes currently taking shape

My current research is developing a coherent programme linking sexual and reproductive health, demographic timing, households, kinship and healthy ageing.

Relationship Context and Contraceptive Use

Examining how partnership status, relationship context and household circumstances shape contraceptive use among adolescent girls and young women.

Timing of Sexual Debut

Applying survival and demographic methods to understand differentials in the timing of first sexual intercourse among young people in Southern and Sub-Saharan Africa.

Pooled SADC Analyses

Comparative population-health analyses using harmonised Demographic and Health Survey data across Southern African countries.

Households, Kinship and Healthy Ageing

Building a longer-term research programme on older-person households, kinship structures, multimorbidity, disability, care dependence and social protection.

Methods & Data

Demographic evidence, quantitative analysis and reproducible research

My methodological approach combines demographic reasoning with population-health analysis, using nationally representative datasets and transparent quantitative workflows.

Data Sources

Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS), Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys (MICS), General Household Survey (GHS), population censuses and national household surveys.

Statistical Analysis

Survival analysis, multilevel modelling, count regression, demographic estimation, life tables and population-health analytics.

Research Tools

Stata, R, SPSS, GIS and open-source demographic tools, with an emphasis on reproducible workflows and careful measurement.
