About Me

I study how people's lives unfold demographically — when they first have sex, whether and how they prevent pregnancy, who survives childhood, and who lives with undiagnosed conditions like hypertension. Most of that story sits in DHS and MICS datasets across Southern Africa, and my work is largely about coaxing it out with the right survival model, spatial map, or analytical framework.

I trained in demography, cut my teeth at Statistics South Africa, and completed doctoral work at the University of the Western Cape. I now supervise Master's and Doctoral students in the Department of Population Studies and Demography at NWU Mahikeng, collaborate through the Population and Health Research Entity (PHRE), and run MT Research & Demographic Consultancy.

Research Programme: Population Health Across the Life Course in Sub-Saharan Africa

My overarching research programme investigates how demographic processes, household structures, and social inequalities shape health across the life course in sub-Saharan Africa. Households and kinship networks serve as the connective tissue linking demographic changes to population-health outcomes.

1. Maternal, Reproductive & Early-Life Health

Investigating inequalities in healthcare utilisation, child survival, and maternal outcomes in Southern and West Africa.

2. Health Inequalities & Healthcare Utilisation

Examining socioeconomic disparities, structural determinants, and biomarker data (such as undiagnosed hypertension in Lesotho).

3. Households, Fertility & Population Dynamics

Analyzing family formation, adolescent sexual and reproductive health (SRH), and relationship commitment contexts using SADHS and DHS data.

4. Ageing, Kinship & Healthy Longevity

Focusing on kinship typologies (absolute kinless, structurally isolated, kin-dependent), multimorbidity, and functional limitations among older populations.

Current Work

  • 01. Relationship commitment and contraceptive use: Using SADHS 2016 to build a relationship-commitment continuum — no partner, non-cohabiting partner, cohabiting/married — and testing how it relates to modern contraceptive use among AGYW.
  • 02. Sex differentials in sexual debut: A survival-analysis manuscript on timing of sexual debut among AGYW using pooled DHS data and accelerated failure time (Weibull) models, framed through Life Course Theory.
  • 03. Pooled SADC survival analysis: Extending DHS Methodological Report No. 36 across multiple SADC countries — shared-frailty Weibull models with country-by-covariate interactions for time-to-live-birth.
  • 04. PHRE digital infrastructure: Modernising the Population and Health Research Entity's web presence and developing open-source Stata mapping tools (mtmaps).

Teaching

I teach population policy, quantitative demographic methods, and applied analysis to postgraduate students, and supervise Master's and Doctoral research within the department. Coursework utilizes real datasets rather than toy examples — cohort-component population projections, life tables, and applied survival models.
