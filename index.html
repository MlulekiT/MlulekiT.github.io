Dr Mluleki Tsawe

Population health across the life course in Sub-Saharan Africa

I am a population scientist interested in how population change, social inequalities, households and health intersect across the life course.

My research uses demographic evidence and large-scale population data to understand population health, health inequalities and demographic processes across Sub-Saharan Africa; this includes maternal and reproductive health and early-life outcomes to adult health, ageing, disability, and healthy longevity.

Research programme

Understanding populations through health, households and change

My work sits at the intersection of demography, population health and health inequalities, with particular attention to the ways households, social relationships and population processes shape health across the life course.

Central Programme Question

How do demographic processes, household environments and social inequalities shape health and wellbeing across the life course in Africa?

01

Maternal, reproductive & early-life health

Maternal healthcare utilisation, sexual and reproductive health, adolescent health, newborn care and early-life outcomes across Sub-Saharan Africa.

Guiding question: How do reproductive, maternal and early-life experiences shape health trajectories throughout the life course?
02

Health inequalities & population health

Social and spatial inequalities in health, healthcare access, service utilisation and population vulnerability.

Guiding question: Why do health inequalities persist, and how do social, economic and geographic conditions influence population health?
03

Households, ageing & healthy longevity

Kinship, living arrangements, household structure, multimorbidity, disability and the social context of ageing.

Guiding question: How do households and social relationships shape vulnerability, care and wellbeing in later life?
04

Population dynamics, policy & development

Fertility, mortality, migration, population change and population policy, examined using demographic evidence from African populations.

Guiding question: How can demographic evidence explain population change and inform equitable and sustainable development?

Current work

Questions shaping the next phase of the research programme

My current work examines how population change, household relationships and health interact across the life course. The themes below are connected rather than separate areas of research: demographic change shapes household structures; households and kinship organise care, resources and vulnerability; and these social contexts influence health, disability and ageing.

This programme brings together demography, population health and household analysis to understand how people experience demographic and health transitions within changing social environments, with a particular focus on South Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa.

Scholarship

Evidence-driven population research in Sub-Saharan Africa

My scholarship is grounded in population-level evidence, demographic thinking and quantitative analysis. I work primarily with nationally representative surveys, censuses and other large-scale population datasets to examine patterns of population health, inequality and demographic change.

Across my work, I am interested in what demographic evidence reveals about unequal health experiences, changing populations and the social contexts in which people live. This includes research on maternal and reproductive health, health inequalities, households and ageing, as well as broader questions of population dynamics.

Increasingly, my scholarship brings these strands together: bringing demographic thinking into population health, and bringing population structure, households and intergenerational relationships into the study of health across the life course.

Methods & data

Demographic questions require demographic evidence

My research uses quantitative and demographic methods to understand population patterns, inequalities and change. Methods are selected according to the research question, the structure of the population process and the characteristics of the available data.

I place particular emphasis on measurement, data quality and contextual interpretation, recognising that robust population research depends not only on sophisticated analysis but also on understanding how population data are collected, defined and constructed.

01

Data & Evidence Sources

Core empirical foundations, nationally representative datasets, and rigorous measurement validation principles.

  • DHS: Demographic and Health Surveys for cross-national health and fertility indicators.
  • MICS: Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys for child and maternal welfare monitoring.
  • GHS: General Household Survey for living conditions and socio-economic dynamics.
  • Census Data: Population and housing census records for broad demographic structures.
  • Measurement & Validation: Questionnaire-first interpretation, metadata and variable assessment, official-report cross-checking, and reproducible workflows.
02

Analytical Methods

Advanced statistical and demographic techniques applied to evaluate population dynamics and health patterns.

  • Multilevel modelling: Examining variation and nested structures across individuals, households, and geographic contexts.
  • Survival analysis: Investigating timing, duration, and transition rates across life-course events.
  • Spatial analysis: Mapping and evaluating geographic patterns and regional inequalities in health outcomes.
  • Demographic estimation: Quantifying fertility, mortality, and migration for planning.

Teaching

Teaching demography through evidence, methods and population change

My teaching connects demographic concepts and population processes with the practical skills required to conduct social research, analyse population data and interpret demographic evidence.

Teaching spans social research methods, demographic analysis, population projections, population policy and applied population research, with an emphasis on rigorous research design, appropriate methods and evidence-based interpretation.

Teaching philosophy

My teaching is student-centred, constructivist and pragmatic, connecting demographic concepts and methods to authentic population, health and development problems.

I encourage students to move beyond learning concepts in isolation and to develop the ability to ask meaningful questions, work critically with population data, select appropriate methods and interpret evidence within its social and demographic context.

The aim is not simply to teach students how to analyse data, but to develop independent, critical and evidence-oriented population researchers.

Profile

Academic profile

I am a Senior Lecturer in Population Studies and Demography at North-West University, where I work at the intersection of demography, population health and social research, with a focus on South Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa.

My academic work has evolved from an earlier focus on maternal and reproductive health toward a broader population-health programme examining how demographic processes, social inequalities, households and health intersect across the life course. This trajectory increasingly brings together questions of population change, household and kinship relations, ageing, disability and healthy longevity.

I am particularly interested in using large-scale population data to produce evidence that is methodologically rigorous, substantively meaningful and attentive to the contexts in which African populations live. My approach combines demographic reasoning with quantitative analysis, with careful attention to measurement, data quality and interpretation.

Before joining academia, I worked at Statistics South Africa, gaining practical experience with census and large-scale survey data. This experience continues to inform my approach to demographic research, particularly my interest in how population data are produced, measured and interpreted.
